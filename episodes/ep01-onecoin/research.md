## Case in one line
OneCoin (founded 2014, Sofia) sold "education packages" whose tokens became a "cryptocurrency" with no real blockchain and a price the company typed in itself. It took in more than $4 billion from at least 3.5 million people through a recruit-your-friends network. Then its founder boarded a plane and vanished.

## Open questions / disputed claims
- Total size: DOJ says > $4B (Q4 2014–Q4 2016 alone). BBC leaked documents: > €4bn Aug 2014–Mar 2017; BBC also reports sources saying "as much as €15bn" (unverified); a 2020 BBC piece says "about £2bn ... spent on OneCoin tokens". Script uses only the DOJ figure.
- DOJ revenue figures differ by window: €3.353bn (Q4 2014–Q3 2016, 2019 release) vs €4.037bn (Q4 2014–Q4 2016, 2022/2024 releases). Not used on screen.
- Konstantin Ignatov sentencing date: Bloomberg Law reports Tuesday 2024-03-05; the government's 2024-10-10 court letter lists 2024-03-07 (likely judgment date). Script says "March twenty twenty-four" only if needed (currently says no date).
- Government letter says Ignatov "committed perjury" during the Scott trial testimony; this is the government's characterisation. Not used in script.
- Greenwood plea: DOJ says one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud; Reuters says "fraud and money laundering charges". Script avoids naming counts.
- Ignatova's whereabouts and whether she is alive: rumours (Frankfurt, Dubai, Russia, death) are unverified. Script says only "not seen publicly since" (DOJ/FBI wording) and that she remains wanted.
- Mark Scott's appeal: a Reuters search result (2025-02-03) says the 2nd Circuit upheld his conviction; that page was NOT opened, so it is not used.
- OneCoin always denied wrongdoing (BBC 2019); FBI/State language about Ignatova is "alleged" — she has not been tried. Script attributes claims to prosecutors/courts.
- FCA notice was a consumer WARNING, not an enforcement action or fine.
- Package prices: BBC €140–€118,000; US civil complaint (not opened) €110–€118,100. Script uses the BBC figures with BBC as source.
